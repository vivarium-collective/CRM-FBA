Q When a glucose specialist and an acetate specialist share a single glucose/acetate pool with no spatial separation, does CRM-constrained dynamic FBA reproduce niche differentiation by cross-feeding — i.e. does the glucose specialist's acetate overflow sustain distinct, temporally offset growth of the acetate specialist — rather than simple competitive exclusion?

Claim

The glucose specialist's acetate overflow sustains a distinct acetate specialist: 93% of the acetate specialist's biomass gain occurs after the acetate pool peaks, and glucose is exhausted exactly at the peak (t=8.3) — niche differentiation by cross-feeding, not competitive exclusion.

Two E. coli populations sit in the same flask, drawing on one pool of glucose and acetate. They are not different organisms — both run the same core metabolic network — but they are tuned to different niches. One is a glucose specialist: it takes up glucose fast, but its oxygen supply is capped, so like real E. coli growing quickly it cannot fully burn the sugar and spills the excess carbon out as acetate. The other is an acetate specialist: it is poor at glucose but avid for acetate. Run forward, the community sorts itself into two acts. In the first, the glucose specialist blooms, multiplying about 116-fold while it strips the glucose to nothing by t=8.3. As it grows it pumps acetate into the shared pool, which climbs to a transient peak of 17 mM. In the second act the glucose is gone, but the flask is now full of acetate — exactly the feedstock the second species wants. The acetate specialist, which had barely moved during the glucose phase, now grows ~55-fold, and 93% of that growth happens after the acetate peak. That temporal offset is the whole point. If the two populations were simply competing for one resource, the better competitor would exclude the other. Instead, the byproduct of the first species' metabolism becomes the niche of the second — cross-feeding. The model reproduces this from nothing more than two Monod uptake tunings, an oxygen cap, and a shared FBA pool, which is why it is a compact but complete demonstration of what the CRM-FBA composite can do.

Simulate the two-species crm_fba_community composite on the E. coli core GSM and measure per-species biomass and the shared glucose/acetate pools to determine whether the acetate specialist's growth is sustained by the glucose specialist's acetate overflow (niche differentiation) rather than by direct competition for glucose.
